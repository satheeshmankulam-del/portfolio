The Post That
Never Appeared

A quiet UX failure inside LinkedIn's content publishing flow — where a successful submission triggers silence instead of confirmation, and users are left wondering if anything happened at all.

Product
LinkedIn
Type
Feedback Design Audit
Focus
Post-action feedback
Published
Medium · Design Bootcamp
01 — Problem

You posted. But
did anything happen?

The absence of feedback is itself a form of feedback — and it's always bad feedback.

You write a LinkedIn post. You tap the 'Post' button. The screen transitions back to your feed. Your post is not visible. There's no confirmation toast. No success message. No indication it's being processed. Just the feed — unchanged. This is a classic feedback gap. The user completed an action but the product provides no acknowledgement. The result is anxiety, repeated tapping, and broken trust.

02 — Research

Why feedback gaps
are uniquely damaging

🔍
Uncertainty creates anxiety
When a high-effort action receives no confirmation, users immediately question whether it worked. Did it post? Did it fail silently? Should they try again?
💡
Leads to duplicate posts
Without confirmation, many users tap 'Post' again — resulting in duplicate posts. LinkedIn's algorithmic processing delay amplifies this significantly.
📐
Trust damage at scale
LinkedIn's professional context makes this worse. A user posting career updates or company news needs to know their content went through. The stakes are higher here.
⚡
Easy fix, significant impact
A simple post-submission toast with 'Your post is live — view it here' would eliminate virtually all the anxiety this gap creates. The technical lift is minimal.
03 — Design Process

Closing the
feedback gap

01
Research & audit existing patterns
Mapped the current user flow, identified friction points, and documented where users encounter problems through direct observation and competitive analysis.
02
Define the core design problem
Synthesised research into a clear problem statement. Identified the highest-impact intervention point where a design change would most reduce friction.
03
Design the solution
Created wireframes and interaction models that addressed the core problem. Iterated through multiple approaches, selecting the one with the best clarity-to-complexity ratio.
04
Document & publish findings
Compiled the analysis, before/after comparisons, and design rationale into a published case study on Medium in the Design Bootcamp publication.
04 — Outcomes

The principle behind
the fix

1
Toast notification
eliminates all doubt
0
Screens that should
ever be silent post-action
∞
Users who deserve to
know their action worked

Every user action deserves an acknowledgement. Whether it's posting content, saving a form, or completing a purchase — silence is never neutral. It's always read as ambiguity, and ambiguity is always a UX failure.
