Designing for
'I Don't Care' Users

What happens when your user has zero interest in engaging? A study of low-intent behaviour patterns and how design creates just enough pull — without tipping into manipulation.

Type
Behavioural UX Research
Focus
Engagement · Intent
Tools

01 — Problem

Most UX assumes
users want to be there

Designing for 'I don't care' users isn't about tricking them. It's about creating just enough friction to pause the scroll — and enough value to make them glad they did.

Product design almost always assumes a motivated user — someone who arrived with intent, knows what they want, and is ready to engage. But a significant portion of real users are passive: they opened the app out of habit, scrolled without purpose, or arrived via a notification they barely registered.

02 — Research

Understanding
low-intent behaviour

🔍
The Scroller — habits without goals
Opens the app because they always open the app. No specific intent. Will leave within 30 seconds unless something creates a pattern interrupt.
💡
The Browser — ambient awareness
Has a vague interest in the category but no specific need today. Will engage with content that feels like ambient value — easy to consume, low commitment.
📐
The Returner — forgotten intent
Was engaged once, lost the habit. Returns occasionally but needs a re-onboarding moment that reconnects them to the value they once found.
⚡
The manipulation line
Low-intent design sits dangerously close to dark patterns. The difference: dark patterns extract engagement through anxiety. Good design creates it through genuine value.
03 — Design Process

Nudge without
manipulation

01
Identify the core friction point
Mapped the user journey to find exactly where — and why — the experience breaks down. Focused on the highest-impact moment rather than trying to fix everything at once.
02
Research existing patterns & alternatives
Studied how comparable products handle the same scenario. Identified design patterns that reduce the friction without adding cognitive complexity.
03
Design the intervention
Created wireframes and interaction models targeting the core problem. Prioritised the simplest change that produces the biggest experiential improvement.
04
Publish & validate thinking
Documented the full analysis and proposed solution in a Medium article, inviting critique and discussion from the broader UX community.
