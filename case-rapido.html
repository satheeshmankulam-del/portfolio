Tapping for
Nothing

A UX teardown of dead-end screens in Rapido — screens that strand users with no path forward, and a redesign that gives users their agency back.

Product
Rapido App
Type
UX Audit · Redesign
Focus
Error States · Recovery
Published
Medium · Design Bootcamp
01 — Problem

Tapping a button that
goes nowhere

A dead-end screen doesn't just frustrate users — it breaks the implicit contract between the product and the person using it.

You open Rapido. You enter your destination. You tap 'Book Ride.' And then — nothing. A screen appears saying no drivers are available, with no suggestion of what to do next. No retry prompt. No ETA. No alternative modes. Just a dead end.

This is high-stakes friction. The user has a real-world need: they need to get somewhere. When the product fails to provide a path forward, it doesn't just lose that session — it damages trust permanently.

02 — Research

Where Rapido
leaves users stranded

🔍
No drivers available
The screen confirms the problem but offers no solution — no retry timer, no suggestion to try a nearby pickup point, no alternative vehicle type.
💡
Payment failure without recovery
When a payment fails, users are returned to the start of the booking flow with no indication their booking attempt was saved.
📐
Location not serviceable
Entering a pickup outside the service area shows an error — but doesn't suggest the nearest serviceable point or explain coverage.
⚡
Cancelled ride empty state
When a driver cancels, the screen returns to a near-blank state. The emotional context (stranded mid-journey) is completely unaddressed.
03 — Design Process

Every dead-end needs
an escape route

01
Audit all error states in the booking flow
Mapped every state where the product could fail — no drivers, location issues, payment failures, driver cancellations. Documented what the current UI shows and what the user needs at that moment.
02
Define escape routes for each dead-end
For every error state, designed at least two recovery actions: a primary action (retry, suggest alternative, find nearby) and a secondary action. No screen without a way forward.
03
Redesign the "no drivers" screen
Replaced the blank error with real-time availability context, estimated wait time, an option to expand search radius, and an alternative vehicle type selector.
04
Add contextual awareness to error copy
UX writing matters as much as the UI structure. "Try a nearby pickup point or check back in 5 minutes — demand is high in your area" is a helpful response. Same information, completely different experience.
04 — Outcomes

Design as a
recovery mechanism

4
Dead-end states
identified & redesigned
2+
Escape routes
per error screen
0
Screens that should
ever have no exit

This is a conceptual redesign published in Design Bootcamp. The core principle applies everywhere: every error state is a design opportunity, and every dead-end is a moment to either recover trust or lose it.
